# Court Order Keeps Haiti TPS Work Permits Valid — What July 24 Actually Means

**Read time:** ~1 min

**Excerpt:** A federal court order keeps work permits valid for roughly 350,000 Haitian TPS holders. July 24, 2026 is a Form I-9 date for employer paperwork — not the day your status expires.

---

A federal court order has kept work permits valid for approximately 350,000 Haitian Temporary Protected Status (TPS) holders, providing a critical reprieve for families facing deportation uncertainty. The protection lasts as long as the court order holds. It is not tied to a fixed expiration date.

**So what is July 24?** It is a Form I-9 date, not a status date. E-Verify tells employers to write **"as per court order"** in Section 1 and **"July 24, 2026"** in Section 2. That is a paperwork convention for employer records — not the day your work permit ends.

The extension comes as a federal judge continues to weigh the legality of the Department of Homeland Security's termination of Haiti's TPS designation. The case, *Miot et al. v. Trump et al.*, has become the focal point of a nationwide advocacy campaign led by Haitian community organizations, immigration lawyers, and members of Congress.

"This extension gives our community breathing room, but it is not a permanent solution," said advocates who have been rallying in Miami, New York, Springfield, and other cities with large Haitian populations. "We need Congress to act."

USCIS confirmed that Employment Authorization Documents (EADs) with expiration dates linked to the Haiti TPS program remain automatically extended per the court order. Employers are urged to accept these extended documents as valid proof of work authorization.

Communities in South Florida — home to one of the largest Haitian diaspora populations in the United States — have organized emergency legal clinics to help TPS holders understand their rights and prepare documentation in case the court order is lifted.

**What to do now:**
- Check your EAD expiration date and confirm it's covered by the automatic extension
- Attend a legal clinic in your area
- Contact your congressional representatives to demand a permanent legislative solution
- Sign the community petition at [haitiancommunitycares.com](https://haitiancommunitycares.com)
