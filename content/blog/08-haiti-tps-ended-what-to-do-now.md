# Haiti TPS Has Ended. Here Is What To Do Now.

**Read time:** ~4 min

**Excerpt:** Haiti TPS ended on July 27, 2026, and TPS work permits are no longer valid. There is no grace period. But there are still real steps to take this week — and the most important one is free.

---

Haiti TPS is over. The protection that covered hundreds of thousands of people
ended on **July 27, 2026**.

This is hard news, and you may be hearing it late. What follows is what is
true right now, and what you can still do. Please read to the end — several
of these steps have deadlines attached.

## What Changed

The Supreme Court ruled on **June 25, 2026** that TPS holders could not get
court orders pausing terminations while their cases continued. On **August 4,
2026**, the federal court in Washington, D.C. lifted the order that had been
protecting Haiti TPS since February.

Two things followed:

- **Haiti TPS terminated** effective July 27, 2026.
- **TPS work permits are no longer valid.** This covers Employment
  Authorization Documents in **category A12 or C19**. Check your card — the
  category is printed on the front.

There is **no grace period**. TPS does not have one built into the law.

## What This Means at Work

Your employer is required to **reverify** your work authorization. By law they
cannot keep employing you unless you show proof that you are authorized to
work now. An expired TPS-based EAD is not proof.

If you have work authorization from another source — a pending green card
application, another visa category, an asylum-based permit — that document is
what your employer needs to see. If you do not have one, your employer may
have no legal choice.

This is not your employer punishing you. Knowing that will not pay your rent,
but it may change how you approach the conversation.

## Step 1: Get Accredited Legal Help This Week

This is the single most important step, and it costs nothing.

Whether you have any path forward depends on facts specific to you — when you
entered, what you have filed before, who your relatives are, what has happened
to you. No article can tell you. A qualified person reviewing your history
can.

Use only these:

- **USCIS legal services finder** — free and low-cost providers near you:
  [uscis.gov/find-legal-services](https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/find-legal-services)
- **DOJ recognized organizations** — accredited representatives by state:
  [justice.gov/eoir](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognized-organizations-and-accredited-representatives-roster-state)

**Do not pay a notario.** Do not pay anyone who promises to fix your papers or
guarantees a result. In Haiti and much of Latin America a *notaire* is a
lawyer. In the United States a notary is not, and cannot give immigration
advice. Bad filings can end a case that a real lawyer could have saved.

## Step 2: Understand the Asylum Clock — It Is Running

There is normally a one-year deadline to apply for asylum after arriving in
the United States. Holding TPS is treated as an **extraordinary circumstance**
that can excuse missing it.

But that exception has a limit. The rules require filing within a
**"reasonable period"** after the circumstance ends. Your TPS ended on July
27, 2026. That period is running now.

Nobody can tell you exactly how long "reasonable" is — it depends on your
facts and who reviews your case. What is clear is that waiting makes it
harder. Raise this with a lawyer at your first appointment, by name.

## Step 3: Ask About Every Other Pathway

Bring these up specifically. Some may not apply to you; a lawyer will know
which are worth pursuing:

| Pathway | Who it may fit |
|---|---|
| **Asylum** | Fear of persecution in Haiti — see the clock above |
| **Family petition** | A U.S. citizen or green card holder relative |
| **Employment-based** | An employer willing to sponsor you |
| **U visa** | Victims of certain crimes who helped law enforcement |
| **VAWA** | Survivors of abuse by a citizen or resident spouse or parent |
| **Pending applications** | Anything already filed may carry its own protection |

USCIS also publishes an eligibility tool at
[uscis.gov/forms/explore-my-options](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/explore-my-options).
Use it to prepare questions, not to make decisions.

## Step 4: Get Your Documents Together

Do this even while you wait for an appointment:

- Your EAD, front and back
- Every I-797 approval notice you have received
- Proof of continuous presence — leases, pay stubs, tax returns, school and
  medical records, utility bills
- Passports and birth certificates for you and your children
- The date you entered the United States, as exactly as you can establish it

Keep paper copies and digital copies in two places. Almost every pathway above
requires proving your history, and records get harder to find under pressure.

## Step 5: Before You Consider Leaving

USCIS has said that people without another lawful basis to stay should
prepare to depart. Please talk to a lawyer before acting on that.

Leaving the United States can trigger bars on returning — sometimes for years
— depending on how long you have been here without status. Departing can
also end applications that might otherwise have protected you. This is exactly
the kind of decision that should not be made from a headline.

## What Is Still Moving

**HR1689** would require DHS to designate Haiti for TPS through January 2029.
The House passed it **224–204** in April 2026 with bipartisan support. It has
not passed the Senate, where an identical bill, **S.4814**, was introduced in
June.

Congressional action is the only thing that would restore TPS broadly. Calling
your Senators still matters. But please do not wait on it — build your own
case now, and treat legislation as a possibility rather than a plan.

## You Are Not Alone in This

Hundreds of thousands of people received this same news. Community
organizations, legal clinics, churches, and mutual aid networks are organizing
around it. Reach out to them. Isolation makes every part of this harder, and
the people around you often know which local clinic is actually answering the
phone.
